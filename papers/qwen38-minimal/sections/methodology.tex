\subsection{Data, optimization, and evaluation}
Positive rows supervise the exact object completion. Entity-only close-name
contrasts supervise abstention, and rehearsal rows protect facts the baseline
already answered. Before optimizer creation, a guard checked rehearsal and
checkpoint-control performance. The generated tables contain the exact row
counts, guard result, optimizer configuration, and completed horizon.
\citep{qwen38data}\claimsource{metadata}\claimsource{evaluation}

Training and checkpoint-selection prompts are separate. However, their
control facts are not completely disjoint: a triangle fact appears in both
rehearsal and checkpoint controls. Final prompts never enter training or
selection. They form a training-disjoint regression suite, not a pristine
research holdout, because aggregate earlier results informed recipe design.
\citep{qwen38data}\claimsource{audit}

Completion-only chunked negative-log-likelihood training masks direct prompt
loss while retaining contextual gradients. The full declared horizon is run;
there is no first-perfect early stop. Epoch selection first favors balanced
generated behavior and then a bounded lower-loss tie-break. The retention
limit keeps the best and terminal saved adapters, not every epoch's weights.
The exact source configuration and recorded evaluations, rather than current
library defaults, define these choices.\citep{trlsft,peftlora,qwen38source}
\claimsource{evaluation}

Baseline, validation, and tuned generation use the same greedy,
thinking-disabled protocol. Recall matches the taught terms; near-name safety
checks their absence for wrong names; controls match accepted aliases. This is
a lexical policy with limited semantic coverage. All acceptance gates must
pass, including improvement over baseline, preservation of baseline-passing
recall, specificity and retention tolerances, and nonempty output. Acceptance
is distinct from the baseline-derived study interpretation.
\citep{transformersgeneration}\claimsource{manifest}\claimsource{evaluation}
