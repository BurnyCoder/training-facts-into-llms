\section{Question and hypothesis}
\label{sec:introduction}
The bounded question is whether a small language-only LoRA update can teach
the synthetic relation under a fixed generation protocol without transferring
the answer to close names or losing baseline-passing controls. The operational
hypothesis was that positive phrasings, entity-only contrasts, and rehearsal
would suffice for the smallest reviewed recipe. The prior execution commit
fixed the ladder, selection policy, and acceptance rules. A standalone
preregistration with the full requested hypothesis and analysis structure was
not preserved; this retrospective edition does not claim to supply one
retroactively.\citep{qwen38source}\claimsource{evaluation}

The fresh untouched baseline and selected adapter were evaluated separately
from checkpoint selection. The observed final result passed the registered
policy while retaining a recall miss. The reporting layer derived the
\Code{candidate-knowledge-acquisition} label from the failed baseline recall
checks. Neither the label nor acceptance establishes global novelty, absence
of latent knowledge, or a unique mechanism.\claimsource{manifest}
\claimsource{audit}

outputs, resource measurements, and public adapter verification. Every
quantitative table and plot is regenerated from checked-in evidence. The
scientific result does not depend on continuing access to the deleted GPU
